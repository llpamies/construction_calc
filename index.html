What it reads

Notation 6ft 3in · 6' 3" · 5'-6" · 6 feet 3 inches · 6 ft. 3 in. · 5' 6''
No spaces 6ft3in · 12ft6in · 5'6". A number glued to a feet mark is inches, so 5'6 and 6ft2 work too
Numbers whole, decimal (6.5 ft), fraction (1/2 in), mixed (3 1/2 in)
Scattered 6ft, 3in and 1/2 is one measurement — six foot three and a half — and the answer keeps that shape, coming back as 6ft, 3in and 3/4 rather than 6ft, 3 3/4in
In prose Order 3 of the 6 ft 2 in posts reads as 6 ft 2 in. The 3 is left alone

Two rules

  • One measurement per box. If a box holds two, it names both and refuses to guess. an 8 ft wall and a 3 ft door is an error, not a coin flip.
  • A bare number counts only in Amount, where it means inches — so 2 1/2 is two and a half inches. In the text box a number needs a unit, which is why ordinary numbers in your writing are never swallowed.

Rounding applies to the answer, in whichever direction you pick — up for material you would rather cut twice, down to stay inside an opening. A value already sitting on a graduation is never moved by either. When rounding shifts the answer, the tape marks where it actually fell with a dashed line.
